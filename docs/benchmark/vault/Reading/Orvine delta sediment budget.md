# Orvine delta sediment budget

A fictional delta used as a worked example. Two dams on the main river: Marrow Gap (1987) and Kessel (1998).

- The yearly load reaching the delta apex fell by roughly 71 percent.
- Bathymetric surveys of the Kessel reservoir imply a trap efficiency near 85 percent.
- To keep its height, the 1,450 km² delta plain would need about 14 million tonnes of new deposit a year (dry bulk density 1.4 t/m³): more than the river now brings in total.
- The few transects that still advance all lie within five kilometres of the Main Pass mouth.
- Heron Pass has been shoaling for thirty years, and a flood-control sill at the head of Salt Creek lets water in only during big floods.
