# Sleep spindles and memory

Forty-eight first-year students learned 60 word pairs in the evening, slept at home, and were tested again in the morning.

## Results

- Students with under six hours of sleep kept only 71 percent of the pairs they had recalled the evening before.
- Spindle density in stage N2 was the better predictor: each extra spindle per minute went with 4.2 more points of morning recall, more than total sleep time explained.
- How long it took to fall asleep made no difference to retention.
- Recording: a two-channel EEG headband the students put on themselves.
